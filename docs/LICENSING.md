# License scope and comparison

The maintainer selected MIT on 2026-11-14. [LICENSE](../LICENSE) applies to
project contributions only to the extent their contributors hold the rights
to license them. It grants no rights in original Championship Manager game
code or content belonging to others, supplied installations, assets, databases,
trademarks or separately licensed dependencies. Existing third-party terms
continue to apply. Reconstruction and a publication scan do not themselves
establish ownership of any original material.

The following precedents were checked against the projects' own repositories
on 2026-11-14.

| Project | Published license | Relevance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [LEGO Island reconstruction](https://github.com/isledecomp/isle/blob/master/LICENSE) | LGPL-3.0 | A close example of a Windows PC matching reconstruction. |
| [OpenLoco](https://github.com/OpenLoco/OpenLoco/blob/master/LICENSE) | MIT | A permissive example for a reconstructed PC management game. |
| [OpenRCT2](https://github.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2/blob/develop/licence.txt) | GPL-3.0; its README permits later versions | A PC management-game reimplementation and modern engine. |
| [Super Mario 64](https://github.com/n64decomp/sm64/blob/master/LICENSE.md) | CC0-1.0 | A matching decompilation using a public-domain dedication and fallback terms. |

There is no single decompilation-community license. These are examples, not a
survey of prevalence. A project's license label is also not evidence that its
license grants rights in separately supplied original game content.

**Selected: MIT for contributions the contributors are entitled to license**,
with original game material excluded and third-party terms preserved. OpenLoco
is a particularly relevant precedent. GPL-3.0-or-later was the copyleft
alternative illustrated by OpenRCT2. See the
[MIT text](https://opensource.org/license/mit) and
[GNU GPL v3](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.html) for the actual terms.

Review contribution provenance and any copied third-party tooling or snippets
before accepting them, preserve their notices, and identify any material that
cannot be covered. Do not infer that all dependency licenses are MIT. The
publication content scanner is not a license-compatibility or ownership audit.
